I experiment with training generative image models. I demonstrate that modern
techniques are effective at training small image generation models using only
a small amount of compute.

	a chicken, class 7, superb cockscomb

Image generation feels like mad science. Unlike natural language processing,
whose generative paradigm slots perfectly into information theory, images
don't present an intuitive objective. The whole training process is vibes
based. While you can derive metrics like FiD, inception score, and recall, and
precision, such metrics are trivially gamified and the whole field is moot
without some measure of human preference<Footnote>https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28190 Representation Fréchet Loss for Visual Generation</Footnote>.

Regardless of image generation's nebulous validation regime, some techniques
are proveably beneficial.

- End to end losses, assuming gradients can behave
https://arxiv.org/abs/2604.28190 - FD loss - Two stage latent generation with
regularized latent space - Latent diffusion converges much more rapidly than
pixel space. https://arxiv.org/abs/2608.29335 - two stage joint training, highly
regularized latent space amenable for diffusion https://arxiv.org/pdf/2608.16887
- latent to pixel adaptation https://arxiv.org/pdf/2606.24888 - Massive
benchmark paper, DiffusionBench - Register tokens
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16147 - Register tokens

I experiment training a mishmash of these recent techniques to show that the
current research is correct and does improve small scale pretraining.

I use a vision transformer with 16 layers and a hidden size of 768. Following
(https://arxiv.org/abs/2503.10618 DiT-Air), I share all weights between each
transformer block, except for the MLP weights. That is, each transformer block
shares the same normalization layers, qkv projections, attention gating
projections, and attention output projections. The layers contain only
different MLP weights. This roughly quarters the number of trainable
parameters. I use attention gating, and share all AdaLN projections between
all layers. I use five registers, which are added at layer 6, similar to
https://arxiv.org/abs/2605.16147

I train a ViT-S to decode the latent space into the pixel space. It takes as
input noisy pixels, and clean latents, and outputs an x-prediction of the
clean pixel values. The loss measures the v-prediction loss. This is the RAE
decoder. Following RAE-v2, I use the last 4 layers of dinov3, normalizing them
using dinov3's final layer norm, and then take the mean, to obtain the final
latents.

I train all ViT models using packed samples. I randomly resize images so that
their side lengths are between 64 pixels and 512 pixels, and are a multiple of
the patch size. The resize preserves the image's aspect ratios and doesn't
increase the size of the image. I append the registers, add positional
information, and then pack images patches into a single batch, each batch
element contains many different samples. This increases the sample throughput
by about three times. I use attention masking to prevent different samples
from attending between each other. Dinov3 naturally supports processing packed
images and I use the same positional coordinate system as dinov3.

## RAE Decoder

I train a small rae decoder, mostly for visualization purposes. I want to be
able to preview the latents during training and I don't care if the
reconstructions are perfectly accurate.

I train the rae decoder for 200k steps and obtain decent results. I use the
AdamW optimizer and don't tune the learning rate from a simple default of
10,000 warmup steps and 100,000 constant steps at 3e-4, and 100,000 cooldown
steps to 5e-5.

## RAE Denoiser

I train the ViT-L denoiser to denoise the dinov3 latents. This is a harder
task than the decoder. I observe decent results after 500k steps. I trained
for 200k steps on a batch size of 192 and a max image side length of 224. I
then bump the resolution to 512 and train for 100k steps at a batch size of
64. I then bump the resolution to 768, reduce the batch size to 8, and train
for 300k steps.

Additional to the denoising loss, I add a REPA loss. An MLP projects hidden
states from 4th layer of the ViT-L. I use an MSE loss between the projected
outputs, and the ground truth clean latents. This REPA projector allows me to
use autoguidance. I use autoguidance instead of traditional CFG.
